## Research question

Did 2x ETFs look weak in Phase 1 because lower leverage is structurally inferior, or because the frozen VS13 framework is better matched to 3x products?

Phase 1.5 was deliberately bounded to four pre-registered common families across TQQQ, QLD, UPRO and SSO. No Native tuning or parameter search was permitted.

## Main conclusion

The hypothesis that 2x ETFs are inherently too weak to deserve further study is **rejected as an overstatement**.

What Phase 1.5 supports instead:

1. Higher leverage still produces substantially higher CAGR across all tested common families in this 2016–2026 sample.
2. Lower leverage consistently reduces drawdown.
3. Risk-adjusted ranking is strategy-family dependent rather than fixed by leverage alone.
4. The original fixed 13% VS13 stop is not leverage-neutral; mechanical stop scaling materially improves both QLD and SSO without ticker-specific fitting.
5. Therefore QLD and SSO must remain full Phase 2 Core candidates rather than being downgraded to mere defensive benchmarks.
6. There is preliminary evidence for an `Underlying × Leverage × Risk-Control` interaction, which is exactly what Phase 2 should test for robustness rather than optimize further in-sample.

## Bias / evidence classification

This phase was proposed after observing Phase 1. It is therefore **diagnostic, contaminated evidence**, not independent confirmation.

No claim should be made that the leverage-scaled stop is a superior production rule based on this result. The correct next step is OOS / Walk-Forward / stress validation under a frozen Phase 2 protocol.
